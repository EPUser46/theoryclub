Recommended Cornell Courses

The Department of Computer Science and Department of Mathematics offer world-class coursework spanning foundational to graduate-level TCS:

Course Title Description & Relevance
CS 2800 / 2802 Discrete Structures (Honors) Foundations of proof techniques, set theory, graph theory, relations, combinatorics, and discrete probability. Essential starting point for all TCS coursework.
CS 4820 Introduction to Analysis of Algorithms Core undergraduate algorithms covering greedy methods, divide-and-conquer, dynamic programming, network flow, NP-completeness, and approximation algorithms.
CS 4810 Introduction to Theory of Computing Automata theory, regular and context-free languages, Turing machines, computability, the halting problem, and complexity classes (P, NP).
CS 4830 Introduction to Cryptography Mathematical foundations of secret-key and public-key cryptography, pseudorandom generators, one-way functions, zero-knowledge, and lattice cryptography.
CS 4850 Mathematical Foundations for Info Age High-dimensional data geometry, random walks, singular value decomposition (SVD), random graphs, and spectral graph theory.
CS 6810 Theory of Computing (Graduate) Advanced computational complexity: circuits, interactive proofs, PCP theorem, hardness of approximation, and communication complexity.
CS 6820 Analysis of Algorithms (Graduate) Advanced algorithmic techniques: randomized algorithms, linear and semidefinite programming, continuous optimization, and online algorithms.
MATH 4710 / 4720 Basic Probability / Statistics Rigorous foundations in continuous and discrete probability essential for randomized algorithms and learning theory.

Cornell Theory Group

The Cornell Theory of Computing Group is one of the oldest and most distinguished theoretical computer science groups in the world. Faculty and students conduct groundbreaking research across algorithms, complexity theory, algorithmic game theory, cryptography, computational biology, learning theory, and quantum computing.
